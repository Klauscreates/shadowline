How Shadowline works

What you see. A 512 km wide map of the Moon's south pole built from NASA's Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter elevation data. Heights are stretched 3× so craters read clearly.

Live light. For the chosen moment, the app works out where the Sun and Earth sit in the Moon's sky and traces a ray from every point of the map towards them. If terrain blocks the ray, that point is in shadow. The Moon's curve is included. The Sun is treated as a disk, so shadow edges are soft.

Heat maps. The same test is repeated across the whole time window and added up: share of time in sunlight, share of time Earth is above the horizon, and both together.

Site test. A click measures the horizon in 720 directions around that spot, then steps through the window to find sunlight, the longest dark stretch, Earth contact, and the longest loss of contact.

Score. 45% sunlight share + 35% Earth-contact share + 20% gentle slope (full marks under 5°, none over 15°). Sites steeper than 15° are halved. It is a quick comparison aid, not a mission rule.

Limits. The terrain grid is 250 m per pixel, so boulders, small craters and local ridges are not seen. "Earth link" means the centre of Earth is above the terrain horizon for an antenna 2 m above ground; it is not a radio link budget. Sun and Earth positions agree with NASA SPICE (DE440) to about 0.004°. This is a research and education tool, not flight planning software.

Data. LOLA south-pole elevation model LDEM_60S_240MPP_ADJ (NASA GSFC PGDA, LRO LOLA team). Orbit and rotation model: astronomy-engine (MIT). 3D: three.js (MIT).
