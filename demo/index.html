At the pole, the Sun never rises more than about 1.5°

  • Every hill throws a shadow tens of kilometres long.
  • A good site needs sunlight for power and a clear line to Earth for contact.
  • Both change hour by hour, and a few hundred metres can decide it.
  • Shadowline lets anyone see that and test any spot in one click.
2 / 7

Live light: real shadows on real terrain

  • NASA LOLA laser-altimeter terrain, 512 km across.
  • Pick any date. Press play and watch the shadow line move.
  • The Moon's curve and the Sun's disk are included, so shadow edges are soft.
  • Runs in the browser on the graphics card. Nothing to install.
3 / 7

Heat maps: a whole lunar day added up

Left: share of the window in sunlight. Right: share of the window with Earth above the horizon. A third layer shows both together, and a fourth shows slope.

4 / 7

Click anywhere to test a landing site

  • Score from 0 to 100, with the reasons shown.
  • Sunlight %, longest dark stretch, Earth-in-view %, longest loss of contact, slope.
  • A 360° horizon view with the Sun and Earth paths.
  • Find best sites scans the whole map and pins the top three.
5 / 7

Checked, not just pretty

0.004°

Largest gap between our Sun and Earth positions and NASA SPICE (DE440), over 134 dates in 13 months.

0.1%

Average gap between the GPU heat maps and an independent site-by-site calculation, over 60 random points.

250 m

Terrain grid size. Smaller rocks and ridges are not seen, and the app says so.

"Earth in view" means the centre of Earth is above the terrain for an antenna 2 m off the ground. It is not a radio link calculation. Research and education only.

6 / 7

Open data, open code

  • Terrain: LOLA LDEM_60S_240MPP_ADJ, NASA GSFC Planetary Geodesy Data Archive.
  • Check data: NASA NAIF SPICE kernels (DE440).
  • Libraries: three.js and astronomy-engine, both MIT.
  • AI use: code written with Claude (Anthropic) as a coding assistant, directed and reviewed by the author.
  • Code: github.com/Klauscreates/shadowline
