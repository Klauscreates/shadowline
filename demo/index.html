1 / 7

At the pole, the Sun never rises more than about 1.5°

  • Every hill throws a shadow tens of kilometres long.
  • A good site needs sunlight for power and a clear line to Earth for contact.
  • Both change hour by hour, and a few hundred metres can decide it.
  • Shadowline lets anyone see that and test any spot in one click.
2 / 7

Real light on real terrain, for any date

NASA LOLA terrain, 512 km across. The Moon's curve, the Sun's disk and Earth's wobble are all in the calculation. It runs in the browser with nothing to install.

3 / 7

Test any site in one click

  • Right now: how far the Sun and Earth sit above or below the terrain.
  • Countdowns: time to the next sunset, sunrise, Earthset or Earthrise.
  • Over the window: sunlight share, longest dark stretch, Earth contact, longest loss of contact, slope.
  • A 0 to 100 score with the sum shown (example weights), a low view from the site toward the Sun or Earth, and the full time series as a CSV file.
4 / 7

Compare sites and launch dates

  • Up to four sites in one table. The best value in each row is green.
  • Launch slip: the same mission arriving on time and 1, 3 and 7 days late. Find arrival windows searches the next 60 days.
  • Finding: at the IM-2 target, a 7-day slip cuts sunlight over a 14-day mission from 97% to 63%. On the Shackleton ridge it stays at 100%.
  • Add a site by clicking the map, typing coordinates, or picking a named site.
  • Find best sites scans the whole map and pins the top three.
5 / 7

Checked, not just pretty

0.008°

Largest gap between our Sun and Earth positions and NASA JPL Horizons over 396 dates (0.004° against NASA SPICE DE440).

0.1%

Average gap between the GPU heat maps and an independent site-by-site calculation, over 60 random points.

250 m

Terrain grid, from NASA's 240 m LOLA data. Smaller rocks and ridges are not seen, and the app says so.

"Earth in view" means the centre of Earth is above the terrain for an antenna 2 m off the ground. It is not a radio link calculation. Research and education only.

6 / 7

Open data, open code, any screen

  • Phone and desktop: the same app, with touch controls, on a phone or a wide monitor.
  • Terrain: LOLA LDEM_60S_240MPP_ADJ, NASA GSFC Planetary Geodesy Data Archive.
  • Check data: NASA NAIF SPICE kernels (DE440).
  • Libraries: three.js and astronomy-engine, both MIT.
  • AI use: code written with Claude (Anthropic) as a coding assistant, directed and reviewed by the author.
  • Code: github.com/Klauscreates/shadowline
